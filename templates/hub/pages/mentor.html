Your role and expectations

As a mentor, your role is to help your mentee become independently funded. That is at the heart of the Center’s mission.

You’ll advise your mentee at every stage of his or her project: you’ll stay in touch, follow progress toward the aims, talk through challenges, and review grant applications and manuscripts before submission.

What mentors commit to:

  • Meet monthlyWith your mentee, all through the project
  • Keep a meeting logEach date and what you discussed, for your report
  • Report in the HubA questionnaire and a report each session
  • Attend the Site VisitIn person or online, May 25 and 26, 2027
  • Review grant applicationsBefore your mentee submits each application or manuscript
  • Review manuscriptsBefore your mentee submits each application or manuscript

Your mentee’s milestones

  1. PresentAt a national or international conference, citing CNAP, during the pilot
  2. PublishSubmit a manuscript to a high-impact journal within six months of finishing
  3. ApplySubmit an external grant as lead principal investigator (PI) within a year of finishing

Appendix A · Pilot Grant Leader Roles, Responsibilities, and Terms

Dr. Amara Okafor – Pilot Grant Leader, Kansas State University

The Pilot Grant Leader shall provide the Services and Deliverables associated with conducting the CNAP Pilot Grant.

Pilot Grant Leader responsibilities to Program Director:

  • Provide quantitative data (presentations, publications, grant submissions, awards and honors, scientific highlights, etc.) for submission to NIH RPPR
  • Prepare research progress summary for RPPR and semi-annual evaluation meetings
  • Complete various questionnaires designed to assess CNAP functioning on a semi-annual basis for the evaluation meetings and RPPR
  • Attend and participate in business and evaluation meetings
  • Attend and participate in CNAP events including weekly brown bag and colloquia
  • Meet monthly with project mentors -- a written formative evaluation will be prepared by mentors to summarize these meetings
  • Cite grant number as follows in all publications that used COBRE project funds or research cores: “This project was supported by a grant from the National Institute of General Medical Science GM113109 of the National Institutes of Health”
  • Send proposal summary statements, reviews, and scores to the Administrative Core’s project manager following each proposal submission

Mentee Rights within the Program:

  • Full access to all COBRE-supported research cores and COBRE-supported programs
  • Use of the mentoring system to facilitate career development

Milestones that will be evaluated:

  • Deliver at least one presentation at a national and/or international conferences that cites CNAP support during the pilot grant support period
  • Submit of at least one manuscript to a high impact journal that cites CNAP support within 6 months of pilot grant completion
  • Submit an extramural grant as lead PI based on CNAP supported research within one year for pilot grant completion

General issues of relevance to the COBRE:

  • The Center Director reserves the right to remove pilot grant funding and/or pilot grant mentors if recommended by the EAC and approved by the NIH
  • COBRE funds are to be used to support pilot grantees to develop independent, extramurally supported grant funding of direct relevance to the project that was approved for funding by the NIH

Sign your mentoring agreement

Your mentoring is set out in a consultant agreement between you and the Center. The agreement’s Scope of Services names you and your mentee, covers the months of mentoring, and lists the services and deliverables described in step 1.

  • Watch for the agreement. The Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts sends the agreement and handles the signing.
  • Please read the Scope of Services. The agreement matches the expectations in this guide, so nothing in the agreement should surprise you.
  • Sign and return the agreement. Your agreement can’t be signed in advance, so the agreement will likely reach you after your mentee’s award has started. Your pay is backdated to your mentee’s start date.
  • Set up payment. The Grant Specialist and Accountant arranges payment for your role as a mentor.

Plan your meeting schedule

Monthly meetings are the backbone of the mentoring. The reports let you follow the pilot as the work unfolds and catch problems early. Set a standing time with your mentee now, so the months don’t slip by.

Keep a meeting log

After each meeting, jot down the date and a sentence or two on what you covered. Your semiannual reporting asks for exactly this: a list of the dates you met and a brief summary of each meeting.

The semiannual evaluation meeting

Each January, the Director leads a meeting on Zoom to review each pilot’s progress. The Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts schedules your mentee’s meeting with you.

What mentors report, and when

Your reporting goes into the Center’s annual Research Performance Progress Report (RPPR) to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), which keeps the Center funded. Center leadership also uses the questionnaire to improve how the Center works for researchers, students, and core users.

  1. Mid-AprilReporting opensThe Member Hub emails you, and reminds you until you’re done.
  2. About three weeksYou reportFinishing early helps the team a great deal.
  3. Early MayReporting closesThe Director, Associate Director, Advisory Committee, and some K-State administrators read the report.
  4. May 25 and 26Site VisitYour reporting informs the Advisory Committee’s review.

If your mentoring also spans a fall session, you’ll get the same two assignments in November, as a status update.

Learn about your role at the Site Visit

The Site Visit is the Center’s main event of the year: two days in Manhattan, Kansas, in May or June. The Site Visit brings together core tours, project leader presentations, a keynote address and banquet, and a poster session. The Advisory Committee also meets privately to review the progress of each project leader.

The 2027 Site Visit: May 25 and 26

See the full program, the map, and travel details

  1. Tuesday, May 25DaytimeCore tours, then primary project and pilot project presentations and updates.
  2. Tuesday, May 25EveningKeynote address and banquet.
  3. Wednesday, May 26Morning, ending by noonPoster session, open to everyone.

You’re asked to attend, in person or online. You’re not required to present. However, every presentation is followed by questions and answers. We also schedule interwoven group discussions. Your input, coaching, and feedback during these periods is extremely valuable. Your presence during Site Visit brings depth and perspective.

If you come in person

  • Lodging is booked for you. The Center books and pays for Site Visit lodging about two months before the Site Visit.
  • Please book your own flight. Flights are reimbursed after the Site Visit.
  • Incidental travel costs are covered. CNAP covers all incidental travel costs, such as meals and rides to and from the airport.
  • Please email in your receipts. Please sign the front of each receipt and email the receipts to roxana@ksu.edu after the Site Visit.

Save the dates: May 25 and 26, 2027. The events appear on the homepage calendar too.
