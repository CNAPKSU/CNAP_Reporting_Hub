The news the CNAP team thinks every member should see first.

  • October 8, 2026K-State Research Weekly

    NIH asks for ideas to reduce administrative burden in grants

    NIH is asking researchers and research administrators where its grant requirements add work without adding value.

    • Comments can cover steps to simplify or drop, duplicated requirements, unclear rules, and reports that are collected but never used.
    • Early Stage Investigators and smaller institutions are named as groups that may carry more of the burden.
    2 more points
    • Responses are due December 8, 2026, through the NIH request-for-information portal.
    • NIH may post responses publicly, so leave out unpublished or sensitive details.
    Read the sourceNIH Guide Notice NOT-OD-26-118

  • October 8, 2026

    NIH reports a record number of R01-equivalent awards to Early Stage Investigators

    NIH made 1,648 R01-equivalent awards to Early Stage Investigators in fiscal year 2026, the most on record.

    • The record came in a year with fewer awards overall.
    Read the sourceGranted News

    Physical activity may protect against depression for people at genetic risk

    Denver Brown, in kinesiology, studies whether physical activity protects against depression.

    • The work looks especially at people with a genetic risk for depression.
    Source: K-State News

  • August 24, 2026

    K-State members can search for funding with Atom Grants

    K-State now provides Atom Grants, a tool that matches researchers to funding opportunities from a plain-language description of their work.

    2 more points
    • It sends weekly emails about matching opportunities and lists each announcement's key requirements.
    • Sign in at atomgrants.com with your eID@ksu.edu address, at no cost.
    Source: K-State News

  • August 11, 2026

    NIH ended continuous submission

    NIH stopped accepting continuous submissions on August 11, 2026.

    • Applications now follow the standard due dates.
    Source: Chapman University research policy updates

  • February 24, 2026

    Early Stage Investigator success rates fell in fiscal year 2025

    Success rates for Early Stage Investigators dropped from 26% to 19%, as applications rose 11%.

    • NIH’s shift to funding more grants for several years at once reduced the money available for new awards.
    Source: Granted News

  • Calendar year 2026

    NIH accepts up to six applications from one investigator each year

    Each principal investigator may submit six new, renewal, resubmission, or revision applications across all council rounds in a calendar year.

    • Plan the year’s submissions with the limit in mind.
    Source: NIH Grants and Funding
