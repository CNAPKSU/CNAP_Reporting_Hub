Before you apply

Weigh every funding opportunity with these seven questions before starting an application.

Please select a question to read the full question.

  1. 1Eligibility

    Are you eligible for the funding opportunity?

  2. 2Preliminary data

    Do you have sufficient preliminary data to be competitive for the funding opportunity?

  3. 3Fit with the funder

    Is your research question or hypothesis aligned with the objectives of the funding agency and of the funding opportunity?

  4. 4Expertise

    Is your expertise sufficient for the call?
    Or do you need to build a team of co-investigators and collaborators to fill potential gaps?

  1. 5Advancing the field

    Will your research question advance the field?
    Does the question sit at the intersection of current knowledge in the field and the next frontier of the field?

  2. 6Available effort

    Do you have available person months of effort for the project?
    Count current and pending commitments, including CNAP effort.

  3. 7Equipment and infrastructure

    Do you have the equipment and infrastructure necessary to make a strong pitch for your proposed project?
    Do you need to source other cores or resources outside of K-State?

Talk through a draft

Unsure about one of the questions? The Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts meets one on one with CNAP members about proposals.

Optional: you’re welcome to bring a specific aims page, a budget question, or an opportunity you are still weighing.

What the funding labels mean

Each opportunity on the Funding opportunities page carries one of these three labels beside its name. The labels show how an award relates to the R01 milestone: independent funding through an R01 or an equivalent award, which every CNAP project leader works toward.

Counts toward the R01 milestone
The award is an R01 or an equivalent award. Winning the award meets the milestone.
Bridge funding
A smaller or shorter award that keeps your research going and builds preliminary data on the way to an R01-equivalent award. Bridge funding doesn’t meet the milestone by itself, but bridge funding can strengthen your next application.
Please ask your program officer
Whether the award counts toward the R01 milestone depends on the award’s terms. Please ask your program officer and the CNAP team before you apply.

NIH opportunities for IDeA states

Beyond the Center’s own award, the National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS) offers co-funding to investigators in Institutional Development Award (IDeA) states such as Kansas, along with capacity-building, training, and Support for Research Excellence programs. NIH also funds research instruments through separate S10 grants.

Opportunity numbers and dates change every cycle. Please confirm each one on Grants.gov or in NIH’s Explore NIH Opportunities tool before you plan around the opportunity. Since fiscal year 2026, NIH posts its Notices of Funding Opportunity (NOFOs) there rather than in the NIH Guide for Grants and Contracts (NOT-OD-25-143).

For an unfunded R01, R15, or R35

IDeA research project grant co-funding

NIGMS co-funds strong applications that align with NIH priorities but were not selected for funding, when the investigators and performance sites are in IDeA-eligible states.

TopicDetails
ApplicationsR01, R15, and NIGMS Maximizing Investigators’ Research Award (MIRA) R35 applications, assigned to any NIH institute.
InvestigatorsA single principal investigator. Applications with multiple principal investigators are not eligible. The investigator and every performance site must be in an IDeA-eligible state.
TimingConsiders applications from the three prior council rounds, starting in May. For fiscal year 2026, those were May 2025, October 2025, and January 2026.
NominationThe institute that reviewed the application makes the nomination.
FundingNIGMS pays up to $350,000 a year for the first two years. The $350,000 is total costs: direct and indirect costs together.

If your application scored well but was not funded, talk with the program officer assigned to the application and with that institute’s IDeA co-funding coordinator.
