Welcome to the Center

The Cognitive and Neurobiological Approaches to Plasticity Center (CNAP) is a Center of Biomedical Research Excellence (COBRE), funded by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) through the National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS). Its overarching goal is to understand the mechanisms of cognitive and neural plasticity and to promote healthy brain function, in human and animal models, through basic and translational research.

Above all, the Center exists to help investigators like you become independently funded. Your award comes with four kinds of support.

MentoringAn expert in your field who advises on the project and your career
Grant writingConsultation and guidance as you prepare your next proposals
Research coresFull access to the three cores, with training in their methods
Exchange networkCollaborations, more core access, and workshops through the Scientific Exchange Network

Being part of the Center

  • Join the weekly NeuroSync speaker series whenever you can. Talks are on Wednesdays from 2:30 to 3:30 p.m. in Leadership Room 127.
  • Attend and take part in CNAP business and evaluation meetings when you’re asked to.
  • Report your work in the Member Hub, during the project and after it ends.

Sign your mentoring agreement

Your award is set out in a Memorandum of Understanding between you and the Center, signed by you and the Director. It names your project and its performance period, states the direct costs of your award, and sets out your roles, responsibilities, and terms, including your mentoring.

  • Read it when the Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts sends it. Everything in it is summarized below, so nothing should surprise you.
  • Sign and return it. The Director signs too.
  • Set a standing monthly time with your mentor for the rest of the award, and share your aims and timeline before your first meeting.

What you agree to

  • Meet every month with your mentors. They write an evaluation that summarizes your meetings.
  • Provide the numbers for the Center’s annual progress report to NIH: presentations, publications, grant submissions, awards and honors, and scientific highlights.
  • Prepare summaries of your research progress, and your progress toward graduating from the program, for the annual progress report and the semiannual evaluation meetings.
  • Complete questionnaires on how the Center is working, twice a year.
  • Attend and take part in business and evaluation meetings. Only excused absences are allowed.
  • Attend CNAP events, including the weekly talks and colloquia.
  • Represent the program at national or regional IDeA meetings when it’s appropriate.
  • Cite the grant in every presentation and publication that used project funds or the research cores (the wording is in step 6).
  • After each proposal you submit, send its summary statement, reviews, and scores to the Administrative Core.

What the award gives you

  • Access to all of the Center’s research cores and programs.
  • The mentoring system, to support your career development. Your mentor is paid from the Center’s central funds, never from your budget.
The terms in the fine printGood to know before you sign
  • Continuing your project through its end date depends on the Center being funded in the next grant year.
  • If your status changes because you graduate from the program, continue, or are removed from it, the agreement ends and a new one may be issued.
  • The Director may replace project leaders or mentors. Replacing a project leader must be recommended by the External Advisory Committee and approved by NIH.
  • Center funds support you in building independent, externally funded research directly related to the project NIH approved.

Review CNAP’s finance expectations and guidelines

Your award and budget are already set up. What matters now is spending within the rules, which come from NIH and the Center.

One yearPilot projects. Primary projects are planned over two years.
May 31The grant year ends. Unspent funds go back to the Center.

What your budget can cover

Equipment, salaries and benefits, supplies, animal care, payments to human participants, other research costs, and travel, as approved in your budget.

The rules to keep in mind

  • Use the funds only for the project NIGMS approved. They can’t support other projects.
  • Any change to your budget or your research aims needs the Director’s approval first.
  • If you receive other funding that overlaps this project before it ends, the remaining funds go back to the Center.
  • You can’t hold other IDeA program funding at the same time, such as another COBRE or INBRE pilot or project.
  • Commit the effort in your award: at least one person-month a year for pilot projects, and at least six months a year for primary projects.

Questions about spending go to the Grant Specialist and Accountant, who manages all Center finances.

Approvals before you begin

NIGMS approves every CNAP award, and your project needs its research approvals in place before any study work starts.

  • Human participants: your Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval letter.
  • Animals: your Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) approval letter.
  • Match the titles. Use the same title on your IRB or IACUC application as on your grant. It makes it easy for NIGMS to see the approval covers this project.
  • Send copies to the CNAP team as soon as you have them.

K-State’s research compliance committees handle these approvals. If your study changes later, the change may need new approval there, and the Director’s approval at CNAP.

Your reporting schedule

The Center reviews each project leader’s progress three times a year: at the January semiannual evaluation meeting, in the annual progress report to NIGMS, and at the Site Visit, held this year on May 25 and 26, 2027. You report in the Member Hub before each one, and the Hub emails you when your reporting opens.

What you report

  • Semiannual progress report: your accomplishments, plans, and any changes. Due before the January meeting and before the Site Visit.
  • Surveys about how the Center works, including one about your mentoring.
  • Publications and presentations that used project funds or the cores.
  • Proposals and funded grants that grow out of the project or rely on the cores.

The milestones you’re working toward

Pilot projectsThree milestones
  1. Give at least one presentation at a national or international conference that cites CNAP support, during the pilot.
  2. Submit at least one manuscript to a high-impact journal that cites CNAP support, within six months of finishing the pilot.
  3. Submit an external grant as lead principal investigator (PI), based on CNAP-supported research, within one year of finishing the pilot.
Primary projectsEight milestones
  1. Set up a working laboratory, including new equipment, and recruit students and staff.
  2. Make progress on the experiments for the project’s specific aims.
  3. Submit abstracts and give presentations at national and international meetings, typically one or two for each year of support.
  4. Submit and publish high-impact articles in quality peer-reviewed journals, typically one for each year of support.
  5. Collect preliminary data suitable for a Research Project Grant (RPG) application.
  6. Submit an RPG application within two years of support.
  7. Build a national reputation in your research area.
  8. Have an RPG funded within three years of support.

Cite CNAP in everything the project produces

This project was supported by a grant from the National Institute of General Medical Sciences P20GM113109 of the National Institutes of Health. The content is solely the responsibility of the authors and does not necessarily represent the official views of the National Institutes of Health.

Reporting continues after your award. The Center keeps asking about presentations, publications, grants, and news coverage that come from the project, because they show what CNAP support made possible.

If progress stalls, the Advisory Committee may ask for more frequent updates and agree on a plan with you, and your mentor helps you get back on track. Talk to your mentor or the Director early if your aims need to change.

Learn about your role at the Site Visit

The Site Visit is the Center’s main event of the year: two days in Manhattan, Kansas, in May or June, with core tours, project leader presentations, a keynote address and banquet, and a poster session. The Advisory Committee also meets privately to review the progress of each project leader.

The 2027 Site Visit: May 25 and 26

  1. Tuesday, May 25DaytimeCore tours, then primary project and pilot project presentations and updates.
  2. Tuesday, May 25EveningKeynote address and banquet.
  3. Wednesday, May 26Morning, ending by noonPoster session, open to everyone.
  • Present your project on the first day, May 25. The CNAP team sends the schedule and format ahead of time.
  • Finish your spring reporting on time. The Advisory Committee reads it before the visit.
  • Invite your mentor. Mentors are asked to attend, in person or online.
  • Bring a poster for the poster session on the morning of May 26, if the CNAP team asks you to.

Save the dates: May 25 and 26, 2027. They’ll appear on the homepage calendar too.
