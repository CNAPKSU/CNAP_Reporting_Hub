Welcome to the Center

The Cognitive and Neurobiological Approaches to Plasticity Center (CNAP) is a Center of Biomedical Research Excellence (COBRE), funded by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) through the National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS). Its overarching goal is to understand the mechanisms of cognitive and neural plasticity and to promote healthy brain function, in human and animal models, through basic and translational research.

Above all, the Center exists to help investigators like you become independently funded. Your award comes with four types of support.

  1. 1
    MentoringAn expert in your field who advises on the project and your career
  2. 2
    Grant writingConsultation and guidance as you prepare your next proposals
  3. 3
    Research coresFull access to the three cores, with training in their methods
  4. 4
    Exchange networkCollaborations, more core access, and workshops through the Scientific Exchange Network

Being part of the Center

  • Attend the weekly NeuroSync speaker series. Project leaders are expected to attend. Talks are on Wednesdays from 2:30 to 3:30 p.m. in Leadership Room 127.
  • Attend and take part in CNAP business and evaluation meetings when you’re asked to.
  • Report your work in the Member Hub, during the project and after the project ends.

Sign your mentoring agreement

Your award is set out in a Memorandum of Understanding between you and the Center, signed by you and the Director. The agreement names your project and the project’s performance period, states the direct costs of your award, and sets out your roles, responsibilities, and terms, including your mentoring.

  • Read the agreement when the Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts sends it to you. Everything in the agreement is summarized below, so nothing should surprise you.
  • Sign and return the agreement. The Director signs too.
  • Set a standing monthly time with your mentor for the rest of the award, and share your aims and timeline before your first meeting.

What you agree to

  • Meet every month with your mentors. Your mentors write an evaluation that summarizes your meetings.
  • Provide the numbers for the Center’s annual progress report to NIH: presentations, publications, grant submissions, awards and honors, and scientific highlights.
  • Prepare a summary of your research progress for the annual progress report and the semiannual evaluation meetings. Primary project leaders also summarize their progress toward graduating from the program.
  • Complete questionnaires on how the Center is working, twice a year.
See all commitments
  • Attend and take part in business and evaluation meetings. For primary projects, only excused absences are allowed.
  • Attend CNAP events, including the weekly talks and colloquia.
  • Cite the grant in every publication that used project funds or the research cores. Primary projects cite the grant in presentations too (the wording is in step 6).
  • After each proposal you submit, send the proposal’s summary statement, reviews, and scores to the Administrative Core.
  • Primary projects only: represent the program at national or regional Institutional Development Award (IDeA) meetings when appropriate.

Your mentor has a guide too. See what your mentor is asked to do.

What the award gives you

  • Access to all of the Center’s research cores and programs.
  • The mentoring system, to support your career development. Your mentor is paid from the Center’s central funds, never from your budget.

The terms in the fine printGood to know before you sign
  • Continuing your project through the project’s end date depends on the Center being funded in the next grant year.

Review CNAP’s finance expectations and guidelines

Your award and budget are already set up. What matters now is spending within the rules, which come from NIH and the Center.

One yearPilot projects. Primary projects are planned over two years.
May 31The grant year ends. Unspent funds go back to the Center.

What your budget can cover

Equipment, salaries and benefits, supplies, animal care, payments to human participants, other research costs, and travel, as approved in your budget.

The rules to keep in mind

  • Use the funds only for the project NIGMS approved. The funds can’t support other projects.
  • Any change to your budget or your research aims needs the Director’s approval first.
  • If you receive other funding that overlaps this project before the project ends, the remaining funds go back to the Center.
  • You can’t hold other IDeA program funding at the same time, such as another COBRE or IDeA Networks of Biomedical Research Excellence (INBRE) pilot or project.
  • Commit the effort in your award: at least one person-month a year for pilot projects, and at least six months a year for primary projects.

Questions about spending go to the Grant Specialist and Accountant, who manages all Center finances.

Approvals before you begin

NIGMS approves every CNAP award, and your project needs research approvals in place before any study work starts.

  • Human participants: your Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval letter.
  • Animals: your Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) approval letter.
  • Match the titles. Use the same title on your IRB or IACUC application as on your grant. Matching titles shows NIGMS that the approval covers this project.
  • Send copies to the CNAP team as soon as you have the approvals.

K-State’s research compliance committees handle these approvals. If your study changes later, the change may need new approval there, and the Director’s approval at CNAP.

You report in the Member Hub before each one, and the Hub emails you when your reporting opens.

What you report

  • Surveys about how the Center works, including one about your mentoring.
  • Publications and presentations that used project funds or the cores.
  • Proposals and funded grants that grow out of the project or rely on the cores.

Cite CNAP in everything the project produces: the Stevens Amendment

What the Stevens Amendment is. A federal law that applies to every NIH-funded project. Whenever a product of the work is shared publicly, the product must say that NIH paid for the work, how much of the project NIH paid for, and that the views are the authors’ own.

Why it matters for CNAP. Each paper, talk, poster, and proposal that acknowledges CNAP’s grant, P20GM113109, counts toward the Center’s record with NIH. Products that leave the grant out cannot be credited to CNAP in the Research Performance Progress Report (RPPR) or at the Site Visit.

NIH’s guidance on acknowledging fundingNIH Grants Policy Statement, section 8.2.1

Publications, talks, posters, and press releases

Under the Stevens Amendment, every public-facing document must include three statements. In a talk or poster, write “this presentation” in place of “this publication.”

  1. A specific acknowledgment of NIH grant support

    Research reported in this publication was supported by the National Institute of General Medical Sciences of the National Institutes of Health under award number P20GM113109.

  2. An acknowledgment of the level of NIH fundingThe statement gives the percentage and dollar amount of the total project costs financed with federal money, and the percentage and dollar amount financed by nongovernmental sources.

    100% of the total costs of this project ($[your project’s total award]) were financed with federal money, and 0% ($0) were financed by nongovernmental sources.

    Fill in your CNAP project’s total award, direct plus indirect costs. CNAP projects are normally paid entirely with federal money, so 100% and 0% usually stay as written. If you’re not sure of your total, ask the CNAP office.
  3. A disclaimer

    The content is solely the responsibility of the authors and does not necessarily represent the official views of the National Institutes of Health.

All three together

Research reported in this publication was supported by the National Institute of General Medical Sciences of the National Institutes of Health under award number P20GM113109. 100% of the total costs of this project ($[your project’s total award]) were financed with federal money, and 0% ($0) were financed by nongovernmental sources. The content is solely the responsibility of the authors and does not necessarily represent the official views of the National Institutes of Health.

Proposals

Suggested wording when CNAP-supported work provided preliminary data:

Preliminary data for this proposal were generated with support from the National Institute of General Medical Sciences of the National Institutes of Health under Award Number P20GM113109 (CNAP COBRE).

The research cores

If you used a CNAP core, also cite the core’s Research Resource Identifier (RRID) in your methods, right after the core’s name. The RRID lets anyone find every paper that used the core, even without the grant number. The CNAP office can give you each core’s RRID.

If you paid for a core as fee-for-service, with no CNAP project funding, don’t cite the grant number. Suggested wording:

We acknowledge the [core name] ([the core’s RRID]) of the CNAP COBRE at Kansas State University, an NIH Institutional Development Award (IDeA) program resource. No IDeA funds were used to support this work.

Papers with foreign co-authors may cite the grant only if the National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS) approved the foreign component in advance. Otherwise, credit the core with the core’s RRID and leave the grant number out.

Reporting continues after your award. The Center keeps asking about presentations, publications, grants, and news coverage that come from the project, because they show what CNAP support made possible.

If progress stalls, the Advisory Committee may ask for more frequent updates and agree on a plan with you, and your mentor helps you get back on track. Talk to your mentor or the Director early if your aims need to change.

The 2027 Site Visit: May 25 and 26

  1. Tuesday, May 25DaytimeCore tours, then primary project and pilot project presentations and updates.
  2. Tuesday, May 25EveningKeynote address and banquet.

  • Invite your mentor. Mentors are asked to attend, in person or online.
  • Bring a poster for the poster session on the morning of May 26, if the CNAP team asks you to.
