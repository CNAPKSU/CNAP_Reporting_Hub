Your role and expectations

A central goal of the Center is to help junior investigators become independently funded, and to build a group of investigators who compete for peer-reviewed external funding in neuroplasticity research. The mentoring program serves that goal directly.

You advise your mentee at every stage of the project. You stay in regular contact, keep up with progress toward the pilot’s aims, and talk through the scientific and practical challenges that come up. You also review the grant applications and manuscripts that grow out of the pilot before they’re submitted, and you return constructive feedback in time to use it.

What mentors commit to

  • Meet with your mentee every month.
  • Keep a record of each meeting’s date and what you discussed. You’ll use it in your report.
  • Complete your reporting in the Member Hub.
  • Attend the annual Site Visit, in person or online.
  • Review your mentee’s grant applications before they’re submitted.
  • Review your mentee’s scientific manuscripts before they’re submitted.

The milestones your mentee is working toward

  1. Give at least one presentation at a national or international conference that cites CNAP support, during the pilot.
  2. Submit at least one manuscript to a high-impact journal that cites CNAP support, within six months of finishing the pilot.
  3. Submit an external grant as lead principal investigator (PI), based on CNAP-supported research, within one year of finishing the pilot.

Your time is paid separately. The Center pays mentors from central COBRE (Center of Biomedical Research Excellence) funds, so mentor fees never come out of your mentee’s project budget.

Sign your mentoring agreement

Your mentoring is set out in a consultant agreement between you and the Center. Its Scope of Services names you and your mentee, covers the months of mentoring, and lists the services and deliverables described in step 1.

  • Watch for the agreement. The Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts sends it and handles its signing.
  • Read the Scope of Services. It matches the expectations in this guide, so nothing in it should surprise you.
  • Sign and return it. Mentoring and payment start once it’s in place.
  • Set up payment. The Grant Specialist and Accountant arranges payment for your role as a mentor.

In the finished Hub, your agreement and its status will appear here, with a button to open it. This prototype doesn’t connect to the agreement yet.

Plan your meeting schedule

Monthly meetings are the backbone of the mentoring. They let you follow the pilot as it unfolds and catch problems early. Set a standing time with your mentee now, so the months don’t slip by.

Keep a meeting log

After each meeting, jot down the date and a sentence or two on what you covered. Your report asks for exactly this: a list of the dates you met and a brief summary of each meeting.

The semiannual evaluation meeting

Each January, the Director leads a meeting on Zoom to review each pilot’s progress. The Assistant Director for Grants and Contracts schedules your mentee’s meeting with you.

In the finished Hub, you’ll keep the meeting log here, and it will fill in your report for you. That needs a small new feature, so this prototype only describes it.

What mentors report, and when

The Center uses what members report to meet its yearly reporting requirements to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), which keep the Center funded. Center leadership also uses it to improve how the Center works for its researchers, students, and core users.

Mentors report in the spring reporting session. It opens in mid-April and stays open for about three weeks, into early May. It feeds the Site Visit, held this year on May 25 and 26, 2027: the Director, the Associate Director, the Advisory Committee, and some K-State administrators read your reporting before it. If your mentoring also spans a fall session, you’ll get the same two assignments then, as a status update.

The Member Hub emails you when your reporting opens and reminds you until it’s done. Finishing early helps the team a great deal.

Learn about your role at the Site Visit

The Site Visit is the Center’s main event of the year: two days in Manhattan, Kansas, in May or June. It brings together core tours, project leader presentations, a keynote address and banquet, and a poster session. The Advisory Committee also meets privately to review the progress of each project leader.

The 2027 Site Visit: May 25 and 26

  1. Tuesday, May 25DaytimeCore tours, then primary project and pilot project presentations and updates.
  2. Tuesday, May 25EveningKeynote address and banquet.
  3. Wednesday, May 26Morning, ending by noonPoster session, open to everyone.

You’re asked to attend, in person or online. Your spring reporting is part of what the Advisory Committee reads beforehand.

If you come in person

  • Travel is booked for you. The Center pays for your flight and lodging, booked about two months before the visit.
  • Keep your receipts. Rides to and from the airport, meals, and other travel costs are reimbursed after the visit.
  • Send them in. Sign your receipts and email them to the Grant Specialist and Accountant after the visit.

Save the dates: May 25 and 26, 2027. They’ll appear on the homepage calendar too.
