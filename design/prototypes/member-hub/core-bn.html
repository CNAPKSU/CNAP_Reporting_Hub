Behavioral Neuroscience Core

Behavioral research, animal models, and experimental neuroscience support for CNAP. Built in 1981, the only dedicated multi-user behavioral neuroscience facility in psychology in Kansas.

Facilities

  • Colony roomsPurpose-built rooms for small animals such as rodents and birds, with special housing for altered light and dark cycles, special populations, and breeding
  • Procedure roomsBehavioral testing
  • Surgery and wet laboratorySpace for surgical and laboratory procedures
  • Cage wash and storageIn-house cage washer and laundry facility
  • Offices and meeting roomIn a separate block from the animal areas

Equipment

Added since Phase 1 of the CNAP grant, with upgrades continuing under Phase 2.

Surgery
Two dual-arm stereotaxic instruments with microinfusion pumps, two isoflurane rigs, two procedure hoods, downdraft surgical tables, and four heat pumps for recovery
Imaging
Fluorescence microscope, co-funded by the Kansas IDeA Network of Biomedical Research Excellence (K-INBRE)
Storage and preparation
Research-grade refrigerator, −20°C freezer, two −80°C freezers, large autoclave, ice machine, and reverse osmosis and deionized water
Analysis
Blood alcohol analyzer
Husbandry
In-house cage washer and laundry facility

Planned for Phase 2: shared equipment for electrophysiological recording and optogenetic manipulation.

Services

  • Design and running of behavioral assays
  • Surgical and laboratory procedure support, including stereotaxic surgery
  • Animal care and husbandry, including cage changes and cage washing
  • Scheduling of all facility spaces and equipment, and equipment maintenance
  • Facility records for compliance reporting
  • Coordination with the Comparative Medicine Group, the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC), and the Division of Facilities
  • Training and onboarding for new students and staff

People

About 20 years of research with rats and mice, including reinforcer devaluation, fear conditioning and punishment learning, alcohol consumption and exposure, intracranial lesion and infusion surgery, and immunohistochemistry. He oversees operations and supervises staff.

Working with the core

  1. Schedule a tourSee the colony, procedure, and surgery rooms, and talk through your study with the Core Director.
  2. Confirm approvalsYour IACUC protocol must be approved before animals arrive or procedures begin.
  3. Complete trainingThe Animal Care Technician trains new students and staff on facility procedures.
  4. Book space and equipmentThe core schedules rooms and equipment for your study.

For facilities, services, or a tour, contact the Core Director through cnap@ksu.edu.
